\begin{abstract}
Data-driven surrogates of finite element (FE) models can accelerate repeated structural assessment, but they usually return member forces that violate equilibrium and give no indication of when they should not be trusted. This paper presents a mechanics-constrained surrogate modelling framework for the linear response of planar moment frames, built and tested with the open-source PyNite FE library. A graph neural network ensemble predicts all nodal displacements and member forces in a dimensionless, load-case-separated form, so that superposition, homogeneity in the loads and in Young's modulus, and the boundary conditions hold exactly. An equilibrium projection with an uncertainty-weighted metric maps the predicted forces to the nearest statically admissible field, with a provable error reduction in that metric. Uncertainty estimates select the FE analyses used for training and refer doubtful predictions back to the solver. On 7{,}200 PyNite analyses, the surrogate reached median errors of 2.4\% for end moments and 4.5\% for horizontal displacements on in-distribution frames, four to six times lower than ridge regression, random forests, gradient boosting and a multilayer perceptron given the same features. On unseen geometries and load patterns the median moment errors were 7.5\% and 8.7\%, with heavy tails. The projection reduced nodal out-of-balance forces from 4--16\% to machine precision. The ensemble spread ranked errors well for unseen geometries but not for unseen load patterns. Acquiring training frames by their equilibrium residual halved the FE budget needed on unseen geometries, whereas variance-based acquisition was worse than random sampling. The batched surrogate was 10--15 times faster than PyNite per frame, but for such cheap linear analyses its training cost is recovered only after many thousands of evaluations.
\end{abstract}
