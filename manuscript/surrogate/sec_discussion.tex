\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Answer to the research question} Within the design space it was trained on, and on design-driven frames whose parameter correlations it had not seen, the surrogate predicts member forces accurately enough to replace most repeated first-order linear analyses at the level of preliminary design and screening. The median error of the end-moment field is 2.4\% (ID) and 2.0\% (DES), the peak quantities that govern member design (largest moment, largest axial force, reactions, largest beam deflection) are within 1\% at the median, and the forces are exactly in equilibrium with the loads. Displacements are less accurate (median 4.5\% for the horizontal displacement field). Outside the training space the median errors roughly triple and the 95th percentile of the moment error exceeds 20\%; the surrogate is then usable only together with a rule that sends doubtful cases back to the FE solver. The two-stage rule developed here does this with thresholds fixed in advance: it accepts 98\% of the ID frames with 1.4\% of the accepted frames above a 10\% moment error, and it refers most unseen frames, keeping the violation rate of the accepted ones at about 1\%. The answer is therefore a qualified yes: the surrogate can reduce repeated FE analysis inside a validated domain, and the domain check tells the user when they have left it, but it does not replace FE analysis for frames or load patterns that the training data did not cover.

\paragraph{What the constraints buy} The exact constraints contribute in different ways. Load-case superposition and dimensional scaling are an \emph{accuracy} device: they remove the need to learn the dependence on the load intensities and on $E$ and make the lateral and gravity responses equally well conditioned, which roughly halves the displacement errors (Table~\ref{tab:graph}). The mechanics-informed inputs matter even more: a conventional GNN with the same architecture but physical inputs has 2--4 times the displacement error. The equilibrium projection is primarily an \emph{admissibility} device. It guarantees static equilibrium, which a soft penalty does not achieve; with a variance-weighted metric it also reduces the axial-force and reaction errors by factors of 2--5, but its effect on the moment errors is small (0.1 to 0.5 percentage points on average over five runs) and statistically clear only on some test sets. With a poorly chosen metric the projection degrades the moments, so the metric is an essential part of the method. The projection does not enforce compatibility: it slightly increases the mismatch between the predicted forces and the forces implied by the predicted displacements, and that mismatch, dominated by the axial terms, is large. Recovering forces from predicted displacements gives compatibility but loses accuracy and equilibrium, by a wide margin. Imposing both conditions exactly amounts to solving the FE problem; using the predicted displacements as a warm start of an iterative solver did not help either.

\paragraph{Architecture} A StructGNN-style network with storey pseudo nodes, given the same inputs and targets, reached similar accuracy, better for displacements on unseen geometries and slightly worse for moments, at seven times the training cost; global pooling and deeper message passing gave the proposed network the same displacement gains. The architecture of the learned model is therefore not the main contribution of this work. The exact constraints, the uncertainty estimate and the decision rules built on them are model-agnostic and could be wrapped around any of these networks.

\paragraph{Uncertainty as a decision tool} The two uses of uncertainty have distinct indicators. At deployment, the ensemble spread is a good ranking of the errors for in-distribution, geometrically extrapolated and design-driven frames, but under a change of load pattern the ensemble is confidently wrong (coverage of the $\pm2\sigma$ intervals 67\% instead of 92\%). A domain check on simple load-pattern descriptors, which are known before any analysis, detects most of those frames, and a split-conformal bound calibrated on validation data turns the spread into a threshold with a stated coverage. During training-set construction, acquisition by ensemble variance was worse than random sampling, whereas acquisition by the equilibrium residual of the raw prediction, which requires no labels, was the best rule in all five repetitions and reached the accuracy of random sampling on unseen geometries with half the FE analyses. Diversity-aware batch selection did not improve the median errors further but reduced the variation between repetitions.

\paragraph{Limitations} The study is restricted to planar, regular, multi-storey moment frames with prismatic members, rigid joints, uniformly distributed beam loads and floor-level lateral forces, analysed with first-order linear elasticity and a uniform Young's modulus. The design-driven set uses simplified sizing rules, not code-compliant designs, and it shares the topology family of the training data. The graph baselines are re-implementations from the published descriptions with adaptations stated in Section~\ref{sec:protocol}; in particular, the poor performance of the stiffness-residual loss in our Leng-style implementation may reflect our scaling of the residual rather than the original method. The adaptive study is retrospective: candidates were labelled in advance and their labels revealed only on request, which gives exact budget accounting but not the scheduling overheads of a live FE loop. The conformal guarantee assumes exchangeability with the calibration frames and holds only approximately for the in-domain part of shifted distributions. Timings were measured on one CPU with Python implementations of the surrogate and of all three solvers.

\paragraph{Outlook} Natural extensions are a mixed formulation that also enforces compatibility through the member flexibilities, three-dimensional frames, braced systems and second-order (P-$\Delta$) analysis, for which the FE cost is higher and superposition no longer holds exactly, and conformal methods that adapt to covariate shift.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

Equilibrium-projected graph neural network surrogates for the first-order linear analysis of planar moment frames were developed and evaluated on 7{,}500 PyNite analyses, verified against an independent stiffness solver, OpenSeesPy and closed-form solutions. A graph network ensemble predicts nodal displacements and member basic forces in a dimensionless, load-case-separated form, so that superposition, homogeneity in the loads, the $1/E$ scaling of displacements and the boundary conditions hold exactly; an equilibrium projection with a variance-weighted metric makes the predicted forces statically admissible; a domain check and a conformal bound on the ensemble spread decide which frames are referred to PyNite at deployment; and the equilibrium residual of the raw prediction selects new FE analyses during training. The main conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item On in-distribution and design-driven frames the surrogate reaches median end-moment errors of 2.4\% and 2.0\% (five independent runs: $2.37\pm0.03\%$ and $1.99\pm0.08\%$), axial-force errors of 0.6\% and 0.3\%, and horizontal displacement errors of 4.5\% and 4.4\%, four to nine times lower than the best of five tabular baselines given identical features.
\item On unseen geometries and unseen load patterns the median moment errors are 7.5\% and 8.7\% (best tabular baseline 22.1\% and 16.8\%), but the 95th percentiles exceed 20\%.
\item Mechanics-informed inputs and exact load-case superposition each roughly halve the displacement errors. A StructGNN-style network performs similarly to the proposed network, so the benefits of the framework lie in its exact constraints and decision rules rather than in the network architecture. Displacement-only networks with force recovery give much less accurate forces.
\item The equilibrium projection reduces the out-of-balance force from 4--16\% (raw ensemble) and up to 120\% (baselines) to below $10^{-12}$ for every model, reduces the axial-force errors by factors of 2--5 and lowers the median moment errors by 0.1--0.5 percentage points on average over five runs with a variance-weighted metric, whereas a Euclidean metric increases them. It slightly increases the displacement--force incompatibility.
\item A two-stage referral rule calibrated on validation data accepts 98\% of the in-distribution frames with 1.4\% of the accepted frames above a 10\% moment error, and refers 75--86\% of the unseen frames while keeping that rate at about 1\% for the accepted ones.
\item Acquiring training frames by the equilibrium residual of the raw prediction reached the accuracy of 1{,}200 random analyses on unseen geometries with 600 analyses in five repetitions, whereas acquisition by ensemble variance was worse than random sampling.

\end{enumerate}
The framework is best viewed as a reliability layer that makes learned structural surrogates statically admissible and aware of the limits of their training data, rather than as a replacement for FE analysis.
