The ensemble spread ranks the frames by their actual error well on in-distribution frames, on unseen geometries and on the design-informed frames (Spearman $\rho_s=0.81$, $0.92$ and $0.75$; Table~\ref{tab:uq} and Fig.~\ref{fig:uqscatter}), and the $\pm2\sigma$ intervals of the end moments contain 92\%, 92\% and 94\% of the PyNite values, close to the nominal 95\%. It is much weaker for unseen load patterns ($\rho_s=0.66$, coverage 67\%): the networks agree with each other on these frames while being wrong together, the known failure mode of ensembles under distribution shift~\cite{ovadia2019shift}. The label-free equilibrium residual of the raw prediction is a weaker indicator ($\rho_s=0.43$--$0.74$), which is why it is not used for referral.

The domain check (Table~\ref{tab:ooddomain}) flags 0.3\% of the ID test frames and 0.4\% of the conformal-calibration frames, within the nominal 1\% for in-distribution frames, and 49\% of the OOD-G and 71\% of the OOD-L frames. The two descriptor groups detect different shifts: the geometry descriptors flag 25\% of the unseen geometries and none of the unseen load patterns, and the load-pattern descriptors flag 67\% of the unseen load patterns and 10\% of the unseen geometries. The load-pattern check therefore supplies exactly the information that the ensemble spread lacks. All 173 OOD-L frames that pass the check are frames whose random bay pattern happened to leave every bay fully loaded, so that only the lateral profile is new. The OOD-G frames that pass (308) include 84 six-storey frames, 83 five-bay frames with at most five storeys and 77 frames with seven or eight storeys; the domain check alone therefore does not exclude all geometric extrapolation, and Stage 2 has to refer most of these frames. No DES frame is flagged.

Compared with the rule of the first revision, in which the 400 validation frames supplied both the domain threshold and the conformal scores, the separately calibrated threshold is higher (the 99\% quantile of 1{,}000 frames reaches further into the tail of the in-distribution distances than that of 400 frames), so fewer shifted frames are flagged (49\% instead of 79\% of OOD-G, 71\% instead of 75\% of OOD-L, none instead of 20\% of DES). The violation rates below show that the conformal stage compensates for this.

Table~\ref{tab:oodpolicy} evaluates the complete rule on the untouched test sets. All numbers refer to the relative $L_2$ error of the end-moment field, $e_M$, which is the quantity the rule is calibrated for. The conformal bound covers the true error in 90.7\% of the in-domain ID test frames (five-run mean), as the guarantee requires, in 95\% (OOD-G) and 98\% (DES) of the in-domain frames of those sets, but only in 84\% of the in-domain OOD-L frames: for shifted data the coverage is an empirical property and fails once the load pattern changes, which is why the domain check is needed. With a tolerance of $\tau=10\%$, the rule refers 0.9\% of the ID frames and accepts the rest with a violation rate of 1.4\% (frames whose true error exceeds 10\% although accepted). On unseen geometries it refers 73\% of the frames; the accepted frames have a 95th-percentile error of 7.7\% and a violation rate of 1.1\%, compared with a 95th percentile of 22.6\% and 35\% of frames above 10\% without referral. On unseen load patterns it refers 71\% and the accepted frames have a violation rate of 1.7\%. All 300 DES frames are accepted and none exceeds 10\% (95th percentile 3.6\%). With $\tau=5\%$ the violation rates are 2.7\% (ID), 1.7\% (OOD-G, about 20 accepted frames), 5.7\% (OOD-L) and 0\% (DES), at the cost of referring 30\% of the ID frames. The joint rate of accepted violations is at most 1.9\% on every set and tolerance, well below the bound $\alpha=10\%$ that holds for in-distribution frames. The rule cannot make the surrogate accurate where it is not, but it makes the referral decision before the FE result is known and with thresholds that were not tuned on the test data.

Because the rule is calibrated on $e_M$ only, it gives no assurance for other response quantities, and Table~\ref{tab:oodother} shows that this distinction matters. For the force quantities the accepted frames behave as well as, or better than, the calibrated moment field: the largest end moment exceeds a 10\% error in at most 0.7\% of the accepted frames, the axial forces, shears and reactions in at most 0.6\%. For the kinematic quantities they do not: the largest inter-storey drift is more than 10\% wrong in 19--38\% of the accepted frames, the rotations in 6--23\% and the horizontal displacements in 9--36\%, because the moment spread says little about the error of the displacement head. The referral rule is therefore a rule for the end-moment field (and, empirically, for the other member forces) only; displacement, rotation and drift predictions of accepted frames are separately validated estimates (Table~\ref{tab:extended}) without a referral guarantee, and would need their own calibrated score.

Figure~\ref{fig:triage} complements the rule with risk--coverage curves. On the ID set, referring the 20\% of frames with the largest spread lowers the 95th-percentile moment error from 6.5\% to 4.8\% and catches 78\% of the 1.5\% of frames with errors above 10\%. On unseen geometries it lowers the tail from 22.6\% to 15.0\%; on unseen load patterns the spread-based ranking is little better than random (21.4\%$\to$20.5\%), which is why the domain check is needed.
