\begin{abstract}
Minimum-mass design of steel frames is usually carried out against deterministic code checks,
which leaves the reliability of the optimised frame unquantified. This paper presents an open,
reproducible framework for reliability-constrained, multi-objective optimisation of planar steel
moment frames built on the PyNite finite element library. Discrete W-shape sections are checked
against AISC 360-16 with the Direct Analysis Method. The ultimate limit state is component-based:
the first attainment of a member strength limit state in a second-order elastic analysis
($B_1$--$B_2$ amplification of superposed linear responses), not structural collapse. It is
considered together with 50-year drift and deflection serviceability under load, material,
fabrication and imperfection uncertainty. A sample average approximation turns the reliability
constraints into deterministic functions of the design for a genetic algorithm and NSGA-II. The
targets are imposed either on point estimates or on one-sided 95\% lower confidence bounds, always
in addition to all code checks. Every optimum is re-verified with $10^6$ independent samples and
exact binomial confidence intervals. The paper distinguishes a point estimate that reaches a target,
an interval that contains it, and a lower bound that exceeds it. The surrogate is validated against
OpenSeesPy and against full second-order re-analysis of ten designs in the failure tail; it is
slightly unconservative for a three-storey frame.

For seven frames, the deterministic optima had ultimate reliability indices of 2.88--3.66 and
50-year drift indices of 0.89--2.54. With point-estimate constraints (targets 3.0 and 1.5), the
reliability-based optima were 1.1--10.1\% heavier than the deterministic optima, and six of seven
met both targets with 95\% confidence. With confidence-bounded constraints, all seven met both
targets with 95\% confidence at 1.1--11.7\% more steel than the deterministic optima. For the
three-storey frame, meeting the target with 95\% confidence in the full second-order model needed a
margin and 2.7\% more steel. Uncertainty-aware optimisation did not reduce the steel mass relative to
code-based optimisation; its benefit is a least-mass design at a stated reliability. All indices are
conditional on the probabilistic and structural models: frame-wide model uncertainty with a
coefficient of variation of 0.10 would lower them by about 0.9.
\end{abstract}
