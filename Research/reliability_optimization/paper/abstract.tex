\begin{abstract}
Minimum-mass design of steel frames against deterministic code checks leaves the reliability of the
optimised frame unquantified. This paper presents an open, reproducible framework for
reliability-constrained optimisation of planar steel moment frames, built on PyNite, with discrete
W-shapes checked to AISC 360-16 under the ASCE 7-05 load combinations, a fixed code basis used as a benchmark. The ultimate limit state is component-based: the first attainment of
a member limit state in a second-order elastic analysis ($B_1$--$B_2$ amplification of superposed
linear responses), not collapse. It is combined with 50-year drift and deflection serviceability.
A sample average approximation makes the reliability constraints deterministic for a genetic
algorithm. The targets are imposed on point estimates or on 95\% lower confidence bounds, always in
addition to all code checks. Optima are re-verified with $10^6$ independent samples and exact binomial
intervals. The paper distinguishes a point estimate that reaches a target, an interval that contains
it, and a lower bound that exceeds it. The surrogate is validated against OpenSeesPy and against full
second-order re-analysis in the failure tail of ten designs.

For seven frames, deterministic optima had ultimate reliability indices of 2.88--3.66 and drift indices
of 0.89--2.54. With point-estimate constraints (targets 3.0 and 1.5), six of seven reliability-based
optima met each target with marginal 95\% confidence in the baseline surrogate model, at
1.1--10.1\% more steel. With confidence-bounded constraints, all seven did so, at 1.1--11.7\% more
steel; each design also met its three targets simultaneously (Bonferroni), but not all seven
designs jointly. These bounds
cover sampling uncertainty only, and drift results near the target remain sensitive to the 1\% error
of the $B_1$--$B_2$ drifts. For a three-storey frame the surrogate was unconservative. A design with
2.7\% more steel than the point-estimate design (5.2\% more than the deterministic optimum) met the
target on the re-analysed tail samples; for the complete full model this rests on an assumed
heavy-tailed residual model, and its interval is model-conditional, not a distribution-free bound. Uncertainty-aware
optimisation did not reduce steel mass; its benefit is the lightest design found, within the tested search budget, that meets a stated
reliability, conditional on the probabilistic and structural models.
\end{abstract}
