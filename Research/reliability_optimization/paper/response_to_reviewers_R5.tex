\begin{comment}{2 (labelling of the $t_2$ sensitivity result)}
Identify 3.16 explicitly as the $t_2$-model point estimate, unless a lower confidence bound has been
calculated.
\end{comment}
\resp No confidence interval or lower bound was computed for the $t_2$ model; 3.16 is a point
estimate. This is now stated explicitly wherever the value appears:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
\item Section~\ref{sec:fullmc}: the $t_2$ model is introduced as a further case ``for which only point
estimates are reported'', and the change of the indices under $t_2$ refers to point estimates.
\item Section~\ref{sec:strict}: ``With the more extreme $t_2$ residual model, the point estimate is
3.16; no confidence interval or lower bound was computed for the $t_2$ model.'' The later comparison
with the $t_3$ lower bound also says ``the $t_2$ point estimate is 3.16''.
\item Conclusion~5: ``Under the more extreme $t_2$ residual model, the point estimate is 3.16 (no lower
bound was computed for that model).''
\item Table~\ref{tab:tail}: the column headings, generated by \texttt{make\_figures.py}, now read
``$\hat\beta$ [model-cond.\ 95\% CI] ($\beta^{lcb}$), $t_3$'' and ``$\hat\beta$ (point est.), $t_2$''.
\end{itemize}
The Abstract does not quote the $t_2$ value. It describes the complete-model result as resting on an
assumed heavy-tailed residual model, with a model-conditional interval.

\begin{comment}{3 (limited meaning of ``exact superposition'')}
Ensure that ``exact'' refers to the linear-response superposition step, not to the whole surrogate.
\end{comment}
\resp
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.1em]
\item Introduction, contribution~1: ``an exact superposition surrogate'' is now ``a superposition
surrogate''. The item adds that the superposition of the linear unit-case responses is exact within
the linear analysis, whereas the $B_1$--$B_2$ amplification approximates the full second-order response,
with a reference to Section~\ref{sec:fullmc}.
\item Section~\ref{sec:superposition}: a new paragraph after Eq.~\eqref{eq:superposition} states that
the equation is exact for the first-order linear analysis only. It says that this exactness does not
extend to the second-order response, because the $B_1$--$B_2$ amplification is an approximation of the
full P-$\Delta$ analysis whose error is quantified in Section~\ref{sec:fullmc}.
\item Section~\ref{sec:fullmc} and the Discussion already describe the drift discrepancy and the
unconservative three-storey result as errors of this approximation. No ``exact'' wording refers to the
surrogate there.
\end{itemize}
